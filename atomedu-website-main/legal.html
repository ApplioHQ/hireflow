atomEDU, also called atom, Proof Lab, the tool, we, us, or our on this page, is a free mathematics tool for students. It is not a school, teacher, testing service, or substitute for instruction.

Privacy

Proof Lab is a browser-based math tool. It does not require an account. Its solver, step checker, generator, and graph run in your browser rather than sending your math to a remote solving service.

What happens when you use the tool

  • Proof Lab does not create an account or a saved progress history.
  • The equations and steps you enter are processed in the browser. The current tool does not transmit them to atomEDU as part of solving or checking.
  • Loading the site and its Google-hosted fonts makes ordinary network requests. The site host and font provider may receive request information, including an IP address. Hosting logs depend on the deployment.
  • If you email us, the information you include is sent through your email provider and received by us.
  • If you choose Share to Google Classroom, a link containing your graph expressions and window settings is sent to Google Classroom. People who receive that link can view the graph. Do not put private information in a graph you share.
  • If you choose to save a graph to Google Drive, Google asks you to approve access limited to files created or opened with Proof Lab. Your chosen graph JSON is sent directly from your browser to Google Drive. atomEDU does not store a Google Drive access token or receive the contents of your other Drive files. You can revoke access in your Google Account.
  • If you choose the GitHub or Notion export, our integration service briefly processes your graph and the provider's authorization token to create the file or page you requested. It does not store the access or refresh token. An unlisted GitHub Gist is viewable by anyone with its link. Notion page visibility depends on your workspace settings.
  • Dropbox Saver uses a temporary graph-file URL that Dropbox can fetch after you approve the save. Anyone who obtains that URL can read the graph it contains. We do not receive your Dropbox password or access token.

What we do with messages

We use emails you send to respond to questions and investigate reports. Do not include passwords, private student records, or other sensitive information in a bug report.

Younger users

Proof Lab does not ask you to create an account. If you are a younger student, ask a parent, guardian, or teacher before sending personal information by email.

Your choices

There is no Proof Lab account or saved progress history at atomEDU to export or delete. Files you choose to save to Google Drive, GitHub, Notion, or Dropbox are managed in those services; remove them there and revoke access in the provider account if you wish. Copy any result you want to keep before leaving Proof Lab. For a question about an email you sent us, write to hello@atom-edu.org.

Art contest submissions

The art contest is separate from solving math in Proof Lab. When contest submissions are open, entering sends the display name and image you choose to the contest service for judging. We do not ask for a device ID. The contest service uses a short-lived keyed hash of your IP address to limit spam, not a raw IP address in its contest database. Hosting services may still process ordinary connection information, including an IP address. Only the owners judging the contest can view entries before a winner is chosen.

When a daily winner and runner-up are confirmed, all other entries for that day are removed from the contest list. We delete their stored images and retry if storage is temporarily unavailable. The winner and runner-up images and display names may remain in the public art archive. Do not include your full name, contact details, a person's face, or other private information in an entry. The contest is for people aged 13 or older. If you are under 18, get permission from a parent or guardian before entering. To request removal of a featured entry or ask about your submission, contact hello@atom-edu.org.

Scope

Proof Lab handles math within its supported rule families. If a problem is unsupported, the tool should say so. The graph is a numerical exploration feature and is not a mathematical proof.
