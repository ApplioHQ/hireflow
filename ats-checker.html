What is an ATS, and why does your resume score matter?

An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is the software most mid-size and large companies use to collect, parse, and rank resumes before a recruiter reads them. It doesn't automatically reject you, most applications are still reviewed by a person, but a resume that doesn't match the job's keywords, isn't structured in a way the software can parse, or lists duties instead of measurable results ranks lower and is far less likely to be seen.

This free checker scores your resume the way an ATS would, across four dimensions:

1. Keyword match

The single biggest factor. The tool extracts the important skills and terms from the job description and checks which ones appear in your resume, then lists the exact keywords you're missing so you can add the ones that genuinely apply to you.

2. Formatting & parseability

ATS software needs clear contact details, standard section headings, and simple bullet points. The checker verifies your resume is easy for the software to read.

3. Quantified impact

Strong resumes lead with action verbs and back up claims with numbers (percentages, dollars, time saved, scale). The checker measures how many of your bullet points include real, measurable results.

4. Completeness

It confirms your resume has the core sections recruiters and ATS systems expect: summary, experience, education, and skills.

How to make your resume ATS-friendly

  • Mirror the language of the job description, use the exact keywords where they truly apply.
  • Use standard headings: ExperienceEducationSkills.
  • Start every bullet point with a strong action verb (Led, Built, Improved, Drove).
  • Quantify your impact: "cut load time 40%," "managed a $2M budget," "grew signups 3×."
  • Keep formatting simple, no tables, text boxes, or images in the body.
  • Include a clear email and phone number at the top.

What does "ATS-friendly" actually mean?

ATS-friendly is a shorthand for a resume that an Applicant Tracking System can correctly parse, index, and score. In practice it means three things:

  • Parseable structure. Standard section headings (Experience, Education, Skills, Summary), no tables or multi-column layouts in the body, and no text embedded in images or headers the software can't read.
  • Keyword alignment. The resume uses the same terms the job description uses. If the posting says "project management" and your resume only says "PM," the ATS may not make the match.
  • Clean file format. PDF and DOCX are universally supported. Avoid .pages, .jpg, or heavily designed PDFs where text is flattened into images.

Being ATS-friendly doesn't mean stripping your resume of personality. It means making sure the software can read what you wrote, so a human gets the chance to see it.

ATS resume checker vs. ATS resume scanner: what's the difference?

They're the same thing. "ATS resume checker," "ATS resume scanner," "ATS score checker," and "resume ATS test" all describe a tool that evaluates your resume the way applicant tracking software does. Some tools only check formatting; Applio's checker scores keywords, impact, formatting, and completeness together, because a real ATS weighs all of them.

What is an ATS-compliant resume?

ATS-compliant means the same thing as ATS-friendly: a resume that follows the formatting and content rules applicant tracking systems expect. The term is more common in government and enterprise job postings, where compliance language is standard. If a posting says "submit an ATS-compliant resume," use standard headings, include the keywords from the posting, quantify your results, and run it through a checker before you apply.
