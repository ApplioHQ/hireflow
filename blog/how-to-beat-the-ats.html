How to Beat the ATS (Without Guessing)

Most resumes never reach a recruiter. Here is exactly how applicant tracking systems filter resumes, and how to fix yours so it gets through.

August 18, 2026 · 4 min read · Applio

If you have applied to dozens of jobs and heard nothing back, it is easy to assume something is wrong with your experience. Most of the time, that is not it. The real problem is usually an applicant tracking system, or ATS, filtering your resume out before a person ever opens it.

What an ATS actually does

An ATS is software that companies use to manage job applications. When you submit a resume, the ATS parses the text, compares it against the job posting, and ranks or filters candidates before a recruiter looks at anything. Some companies use the ranking as a soft suggestion. Many use it as a hard cutoff. If your resume does not clear that bar, it may never be seen by a human at all.

If you want the deeper background on how these systems read and score a resume, our guide on how to pass the ATS breaks it down step by step.

The five most common reasons resumes get filtered out

  1. Inconsistent or complex formatting. Multi-column layouts, text boxes, and unusual fonts can confuse the parser, which means your information gets scrambled or dropped entirely. The fix is a clean, single-column layout with standard section headers like Experience, Education, and Skills.
  2. Missing exact keyword matches. If the job posting says "project management" and your resume says "managed projects," some systems will not count that as a match. The fix is pulling the exact phrases from the job description and working them naturally into your bullet points.
  3. Tables, graphics, and icons. These often do not parse at all, which means any information inside them is invisible to the ATS. The fix is keeping your resume in plain text and standard formatting, saving the visual design for your portfolio or LinkedIn instead.
  4. Sending the same generic resume everywhere. A resume built for one role rarely scores well against a different one, because the keyword overlap just is not there. The fix is tailoring your resume to each specific job description, even if it takes a few extra minutes.
  5. File type issues. PDFs built from design tools sometimes parse worse than standard Word documents or PDFs exported directly from a text editor. The fix is testing your file and confirming the text extracts cleanly.
