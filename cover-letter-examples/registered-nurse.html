Registered Nurse cover letter example

Dear Hiring Manager,

I'm writing to apply for the Registered Nurse position at [Facility]. As a licensed RN with three years of med-surg experience and current BLS/ACLS certification, I'm drawn to your reputation for patient-centered care.

In my current role I manage a caseload of 6-8 patients per shift while maintaining a 98% patient satisfaction score and zero medication errors over the past 18 months. I'm experienced with EHR documentation, wound care, and coordinating discharge plans with interdisciplinary teams. Colleagues know me as the nurse who stays calm during a code and never lets a detail slip.

I'd welcome the chance to bring my clinical skills and steady presence to [Facility]. Your commitment to [specific value or program] aligns closely with why I became a nurse.

Sincerely,
[Your Name]

How to write a registered nurse cover letter

  • State your license and key certifications (RN, BLS, ACLS) in the first paragraph
  • Quantify caseload, patient satisfaction, and safety record
  • Name specific clinical skills relevant to the unit
  • Show both competence and compassion

Cover letter structure

Every strong cover letter follows the same three-part structure:

  • Opening — State the role, hook with your strongest, most relevant achievement, and show genuine interest in the company.
  • Body — Prove your fit with one or two quantified accomplishments that map to the job's requirements.
  • Closing — Connect to something specific about the company, restate your value, and invite the next step.

Frequently asked questions

What should a nurse include in a cover letter?

Your RN license, certifications, clinical setting, caseload, and a patient-care outcome. Tie it to the facility's specialty or values.

How do new grad nurses write a cover letter?

Lead with your license and clinical rotations. Quantify your rotation hours and highlight transferable skills from preceptorships and clinicals.

Should I mention specific units or specialties?

Yes. Naming the unit (ICU, med-surg, ER) and relevant skills shows you understand the role and can start with minimal training.
