Teacher Cover Letter Example

Updated 2026-09-22 · Applio
A teacher cover letter should show your classroom impact and your philosophy. Lead with student outcomes, then connect your approach to the school's mission.

Teacher cover letter example

Dear Hiring Manager,

I'm applying for the [Grade/Subject] Teacher position at [School]. As a certified educator with five years of classroom experience, I'm drawn to your school's commitment to [specific mission or value].

In my current role I teach five sections of [subject] to 130 students, and last year 88% of my students met or exceeded growth targets on state assessments. I differentiate instruction for diverse learners, integrate technology thoughtfully, and build the kind of classroom culture where students feel safe to take risks. I also sponsor our debate club and collaborate closely with families.

I'd be honored to bring my energy and student-centered approach to [School]. I believe every student can grow with the right support, and I'd love to be part of a team that shares that belief.

Sincerely,
[Your Name]

How to write a teacher cover letter

  • Lead with student growth or achievement data
  • State your certification and grade/subject
  • Show your teaching philosophy in action, not as a slogan
  • Connect to the school's specific mission or community

Cover letter structure

Every strong cover letter follows the same three-part structure:

  • Opening — State the role, hook with your strongest, most relevant achievement, and show genuine interest in the company.
  • Body — Prove your fit with one or two quantified accomplishments that map to the job's requirements.
  • Closing — Connect to something specific about the company, restate your value, and invite the next step.

Frequently asked questions

What should a teacher cover letter include?

Your certification, grade/subject, a student-outcome metric, your teaching philosophy in action, and a connection to the school's mission.

How do new teachers write a cover letter?

Lead with student teaching results and your certification. Highlight classroom management, differentiation, and any coaching or tutoring experience.

Should I mention extracurriculars?

Yes. Coaching, clubs, and family engagement show you'll contribute to the whole school community, which principals value.
