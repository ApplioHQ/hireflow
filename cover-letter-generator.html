What makes a strong cover letter

  • A specific opening. Name the role and one concrete reason you're a fit in the first two sentences; skip "I am writing to apply for."
  • Evidence, not adjectives. Back each claim with a quantified result from your resume rather than words like "hardworking" or "passionate."
  • A mirror of the job posting. Use the same key skills and terms the listing uses, both so a human sees the match and so any ATS keyword screen passes.
  • One page, three short paragraphs. A hook, a paragraph proving fit, and a brief close with a call to action is all a hiring manager will read.
  • A confident close. End by stating you'd welcome a conversation, not by apologizing or padding.

Cover letter mistakes to avoid

  • Reusing one generic letter for every job, hiring managers spot it instantly, and it's the top reason cover letters get ignored.
  • Restating your resume line by line instead of telling the short story behind your best results.
  • Writing about what the job does for you rather than what you bring to the team.
  • Leaving in the wrong company name or role, always proofread the details a template fills in.

Cover letter vs. resume: what's the difference?

Your resume lists your qualifications in structured bullet points. A cover letter tells the short story behind your best results and explains why this specific role is the right fit. The resume proves you can do the job; the cover letter explains why you want it and what makes you stand out from other qualified candidates.

A good rule of thumb: if the resume answers "what did you do?", the cover letter answers "why should we talk to you about this role?" The two documents complement each other, they should never repeat the same sentences.

When do you actually need a cover letter?

  • When the application has a cover letter field. Leaving it blank signals low effort, even if the posting says "optional."
  • Career changes. A cover letter bridges the gap between your past experience and the new direction, something a resume alone can't do.
  • Competitive or senior roles. At director level and above, a cover letter often goes directly to the hiring manager. It's your chance to speak to them before the interview.
  • Referrals. If someone referred you, the cover letter is where you mention it, and a referred application with a tailored letter almost always gets read.
  • When you're not a textbook fit. If your resume doesn't check every box, a cover letter lets you address gaps head-on and redirect attention to what you do bring.

How long should a cover letter be?

Three paragraphs, one page, 250-400 words. Hiring managers spend under 30 seconds on a cover letter, so every sentence needs to earn its place. The opening should name the role and your strongest fit in two sentences. The middle paragraph proves it with one or two specific, quantified results. The close states what you'd like to happen next.

Anything longer gets skimmed or skipped. Anything shorter looks like you didn't try.

Cover letter format and structure

  1. Header. Your name, email, phone, and the date. Match the formatting of your resume for a polished, consistent look.
  2. Opening hook (2-3 sentences). Name the role, where you found it, and one concrete reason you're a strong fit. Skip "I am writing to express my interest."
  3. Proof paragraph (4-6 sentences). Pick one or two achievements that directly mirror what the job asks for. Quantify them: revenue grown, time saved, users served, team size led. If a referral brought you here, mention it.
  4. Close (2-3 sentences). Say you'd welcome a conversation, mention your availability, and thank them for their time. Don't apologize or hedge.

Frequently asked questions

Is the cover letter generator free?

Yes. You can generate and edit a tailored cover letter for free. AI rewrites, downloads, and editing are all free, with no credit card required.

Will the cover letter sound like a template?

It shouldn't. The generator writes from the specific job posting and your real achievements, and you can edit every line. The best results come from adding one or two concrete, quantified wins before you export.

Do I even need a cover letter?

When an application offers the field, a short, tailored cover letter still helps, especially for competitive roles, career changes, or when you want to explain something a resume can't. If a posting explicitly says not to include one, follow that.

Can it use my existing resume?

Yes. If you've built or imported a resume in Applio, the generator pulls your experience and achievements automatically so you don't retype them.

What should I not include in a cover letter?

Salary expectations (unless explicitly asked), negative comments about previous employers, personal information unrelated to the job, or long paragraphs restating your resume bullet by bullet. Keep it focused on why you're a fit for this specific role.

How do I make my cover letter stand out?

Lead with a specific, quantified achievement that matches what the role asks for. Mention the company by name and reference something specific about them (a product, a recent milestone, a value from their careers page). Generic letters get generic treatment.

Can I use the same cover letter for every job?

No. A generic cover letter is worse than no cover letter, because it signals you didn't read the posting. At minimum, change the company name, role title, and the key achievement you lead with. Applio's generator tailors each letter to the specific job description you paste.

How is an AI cover letter generator different from a template?

A template gives you blanks to fill in ("Dear [Company], I am applying for [Role]"). Applio's generator reads the actual job posting, identifies what the role is asking for, and writes a letter matched to those requirements using your real experience, so the result reads like you wrote it, not like you filled in a form.
