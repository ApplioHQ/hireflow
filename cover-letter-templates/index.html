How to write a cover letter that gets read

Most cover letters are ignored because they say generic things a resume already says. A cover letter that gets read does three things the resume can't: it explains why this specific role at this specific company it shows one accomplishment in more depth than a bullet allows, and it demonstrates writing quality.

The four-paragraph structure

  • Opening (2-3 sentences): Name the role and one specific reason it stands out. Skip generic "I'm excited to apply" openers.
  • Why you're qualified (1 paragraph): One specific accomplishment from your resume, in more depth. Numbers, context, outcome.
  • Why this company (1 paragraph): Something specific about the company or team. Show you've done more than skim the About page.
  • Close (2-3 sentences): Reinforce fit briefly and end with a clear next-step ask.

What to skip

  • Generic openers: "I am writing to apply for the position of..."
  • Restating your entire resume in paragraph form
  • Buzzwords: "team player," "results-driven," "detail-oriented"
  • Anything longer than one page

FAQ

Should my cover letter match my resume?

Yes. Recruiters see them as a single document. Matching typography, header, and accent color signals professionalism. Applio pairs your cover letter format to your resume template automatically.

Are the cover letter templates free?

Yes. Every resume template comes with a matching cover letter format at no extra cost.

How long should a cover letter be?

One page. Typically 3-4 paragraphs and 250-400 words.

Do I need a cover letter for every job?

Include one whenever the application allows it. Especially for smaller companies, roles with fewer applicants, and any role where fit or motivation matter as much as skills.
