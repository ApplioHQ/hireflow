ATS-Friendly Resume: What It Means and How to Write One

Updated 2026-09-04 · 8 min read · Applio
An ATS-friendly resume is one that Applicant Tracking Systems can parse cleanly and rank well. In practice: single-column layout, standard section headings (Experience, Education, Skills), simple fonts, no tables or images in the body, and keywords that match the job description. Save as PDF or DOCX. If any of that breaks, your resume ranks lower and a recruiter never sees it.

What ATS software actually does

Applicant Tracking Systems (Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, iCIMS, Taleo, and dozens more) do three things when you submit a resume:

  1. Parse it into structured fields: name, contact info, experience, education, skills
  2. Score it against the job description's keywords and requirements
  3. Rank it in the recruiter's queue alongside every other applicant

The common myth is that ATS auto-rejects resumes. It almost never does. What actually happens: your resume ranks lower because of missing keywords or a parsing error, and a recruiter with 200 resumes to review never scrolls down far enough to reach it.

The 7 rules for an ATS-friendly resume

1. Use a single-column layout

Multi-column layouts (sidebar with contact info on the left, main content on the right) are the #1 reason resumes parse badly. ATS reads left-to-right, top-to-bottom, and merges columns in unpredictable ways. Stick to single-column.

2. Use standard section headings

ATS looks for specific words to find each section. Use these exactly:

Use

  • Experience (or Work Experience, Professional Experience)
  • Education
  • Skills
  • Summary (or Professional Summary)
  • Projects
  • Certifications

Avoid

  • "Where I've Been" (use Experience)
  • "Learnings" (use Education)
  • "What I Bring" (use Skills or Summary)
  • "Career Journey" (use Experience)
  • Anything cute or clever

3. Skip tables, text boxes, and images in the body

Tables often parse as scrambled text. Text boxes get skipped entirely. Images (icons, logos, photos, decorative graphics) contribute nothing to ATS and often confuse it. Your resume should be text on a page.

4. Use common fonts and sizes

Safe fonts: Arial, Calibri, Helvetica, Georgia, Times New Roman, Cambria. Body text 10-12pt. Section headings 12-14pt. Avoid decorative or unusual fonts — some ATS strips unknown fonts and reflows your text.

5. Match the job description's keywords

This is the biggest ranking factor. Read the job posting and note every specific tool, technology, methodology, or skill mentioned. Add the ones that genuinely apply to your experience, using the exact wording. "Cross-functional collaboration" and "worked with different teams" mean the same thing to a human, but ATS treats them as different keywords.

6. Put dates in a consistent format

ATS reads dates to understand your timeline. Use Month Year - Month Year (e.g., Jan 2023 - Present) and be consistent across every role. Don't use just years (2019-2022 leaves gaps ambiguous) or unusual formats (January '23-current).

7. Save as PDF or DOCX

  • PDF: Safe with modern ATS (Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby). Preserves formatting perfectly.
  • DOCX: Universally accepted. Slightly safer for older or unknown systems.
  • Never use: .pages, .odt, image files, Google Docs share links — these fail or parse badly.

When in doubt: submit PDF unless the application explicitly asks for DOCX.

What "ATS-friendly" doesn't mean

ATS-friendly doesn't mean ugly or plain. It means structurally clean. You can have a beautifully designed resume that's fully ATS-friendly — Applio's 18 templates are all built this way. The design lives in typography, spacing, and hierarchy, not in visual gimmicks that break parsing.

ATS-friendly also doesn't mean keyword-stuffed. Cramming keywords in white text, hidden footers, or repeated 15 times in a "skills" list is detected by modern ATS and by any recruiter who opens the file. Add keywords that reflect your actual experience, in the natural context of your bullets.

ATS-friendly resume checklist

  • Single-column layout with clear section headings
  • Standard section names: Summary, Experience, Education, Skills
  • Common fonts (Arial, Calibri, Georgia, Times New Roman)
  • No tables, text boxes, or images in the body
  • Consistent date format across all roles
  • Keywords from the job description in your bullets and skills section
  • Every bullet starts with an action verb (Built, Led, Reduced)
  • At least 50% of bullets include a measurable result
  • Saved as PDF or DOCX
  • Contact info at the top: name, email, phone, LinkedIn, city/state

Frequently asked questions

What is an ATS-friendly resume?

One that Applicant Tracking Systems can parse cleanly. Uses standard section headings, single-column formatting, common fonts, and keywords from the job description.

Does ATS reject my resume automatically?

Almost never. ATS ranks and organizes resumes for recruiters. What actually happens: your resume ranks lower and never gets scrolled to.

What file format is best for ATS?

PDF works with modern ATS. DOCX is universally accepted and slightly safer for older systems. Never .pages, .odt, or image files.

How do I know if my resume is ATS-friendly?

Run it through a free ATS checker. You'll see your keyword match score, formatting warnings, and missing sections in seconds.

Are Canva or InDesign resumes ATS-friendly?

Usually no. Canva templates often use multi-column layouts, decorative fonts, and images that break parsing. Use a template built specifically for ATS instead.
