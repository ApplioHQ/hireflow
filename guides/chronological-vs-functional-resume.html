Chronological vs Functional Resume: Which Format Is Best?

Updated 2026-09-17 · Applio
A chronological resume lists jobs from newest to oldest, it's the standard format and what ATS and recruiters prefer. A functional resume groups your experience by skill instead of by job. For most people, chronological is the right choice. Functional only makes sense for career changers or candidates with significant gaps.

Chronological format: how it works

The reverse-chronological resume is the standard. It organizes your resume like this:

  1. Contact information
  2. Professional summary
  3. Work experience (newest first, with bullet points)
  4. Education
  5. Skills

Pros

  • ATS parses it reliably, job titles, companies, and dates are where the system expects them
  • Recruiters can scan your career trajectory in seconds
  • Shows career growth, promotions, and increasing responsibility
  • Works for 90%+ of job seekers

Cons

  • Employment gaps are visible
  • Frequent job changes stand out
  • Older, less relevant experience still takes up space

Functional format: how it works

A functional resume replaces the traditional work history with skill-based sections:

  1. Contact information
  2. Objective or summary
  3. Skills sections (e.g., "Project Management," "Data Analysis," "Sales")
  4. Work history (brief, titles, companies, dates only)
  5. Education

Pros

  • Highlights transferable skills for career changers
  • De-emphasizes gaps or short tenures
  • Useful for freelancers with many short engagements

Cons

  • ATS often can't parse it correctly, skills get disconnected from employers
  • Many recruiters view it as a red flag ("What are they hiding?")
  • Doesn't show career progression
  • Achievements lack context when separated from specific jobs

The combination (hybrid) format

The combination format takes the best of both: a skills-focused top section followed by a full chronological work history. This is often the best choice when a pure chronological format doesn't showcase your strengths:

  1. Contact information
  2. Professional summary
  3. Key skills or core competencies section
  4. Work experience (reverse-chronological with bullets)
  5. Education

The combination format is ATS-compatible because it preserves the chronological work history that parsers expect, while front-loading the skills and keywords that matter most.

Which should you choose?

  • Steady career in the same field → Chronological. Let your progression speak.
  • Changing careers → Combination. Lead with transferable skills, then show your timeline.
  • Employment gaps → Combination. The skills section draws attention first, and the chronological section still satisfies the ATS.
  • Freelancer or consultant → Combination. Group projects by skill area, then list clients chronologically.
  • Senior executive → Chronological. Your titles and companies carry weight.
  • Entry-level or student → Chronological. Even with limited experience, the format is expected and ATS-safe.

When in doubt, go chronological. It's the safest bet for ATS and the format recruiters are trained to read.

ATS compatibility

Applicant Tracking Systems parse resumes by looking for job titles, company names, dates, and skills in predictable locations. Here's how each format performs:

  • Chronological: Parsed correctly by virtually every ATS. Job titles and dates are where the system expects them.
  • Functional: Frequently mis-parsed. Because skills are separated from jobs, the ATS may create an incomplete profile, your skills appear, but the system can't tell where or when you used them.
  • Combination: Parsed well as long as the work history section uses standard formatting (title, company, dates, bullets).

Frequently asked questions

What is a chronological resume?

A chronological resume (reverse-chronological) lists your work experience from most recent to oldest. Each job includes your title, company, dates, and bullet points. It's the standard format that ATS and recruiters prefer.

What is a functional resume?

A functional resume organizes your experience by skill category instead of by job. It groups achievements under headings like "Project Management" or "Data Analysis" and lists work history briefly at the bottom.

Which format do ATS prefer?

ATS strongly prefer chronological. Most are built to parse job titles, companies, and dates in reverse-chronological order. Functional resumes can confuse parsers because skills are separated from the jobs where they were used.

What is a combination (hybrid) resume?

A combination resume leads with a skills summary, then follows with standard chronological work history. It gives you skills visibility with ATS-compatible structure.

When should I use a functional resume?

Only for major career changes, significant gaps, or when your skills matter more than where you worked (e.g., freelancers). Even then, consider a combination format first, it's safer for ATS.

Is a functional resume a red flag?

Often, yes. Many recruiters view it with suspicion because it's commonly used to hide gaps or short tenures. Address concerns in a cover letter rather than restructuring your entire resume.
