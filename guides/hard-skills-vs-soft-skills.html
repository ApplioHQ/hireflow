Hard Skills vs Soft Skills: What's the Difference?

Updated 2026-09-17 · Applio
Hard skills are teachable, measurable abilities like programming, data analysis, or financial modeling. Soft skills are interpersonal traits like communication, leadership, and problem-solving. On a resume, you need both: hard skills get you past the ATS, and soft skills win the interview.

Soft skills examples

  • Communication: presenting ideas clearly in writing and speech
  • Leadership: guiding teams, making decisions, taking ownership
  • Problem-solving: analyzing issues and finding effective solutions
  • Time management: prioritizing tasks and meeting deadlines
  • Adaptability: adjusting to new situations, tools, or priorities
  • Teamwork: collaborating effectively with diverse groups
  • Critical thinking: evaluating information objectively before acting
  • Attention to detail: catching errors and maintaining quality
  • Emotional intelligence: reading social cues, managing relationships
  • Conflict resolution: mediating disagreements productively

How to list skills on a resume

Hard skills: name them explicitly

Put hard skills in a dedicated Skills section so the ATS can scan them. Group by category (e.g., "Languages," "Tools," "Certifications") and mirror the exact terms from the job description.

Soft skills: show, don't tell

Avoid a bullet list that says "leadership, teamwork, communication." Instead, demonstrate soft skills through your experience bullets:

  • Instead of: "Strong leadership skills"
  • Write: "Led a cross-functional team of 8 to deliver a product launch 2 weeks ahead of schedule"
  • Instead of: "Excellent communicator"
  • Write: "Presented quarterly financial analysis to C-suite, influencing a $2M budget reallocation"

Getting the balance right

The ideal ratio depends on the role:

  • Technical roles (engineer, analyst, designer): lean heavier on hard skills, 70/30 is typical. The ATS is scanning for specific tools and languages.
  • Management roles (project manager, team lead): closer to 50/50. Employers want proof you can both do the work and lead people doing it.
  • Client-facing roles (sales, customer success, consulting): soft skills are critical, but still list the hard skills (CRM, analytics) that show you can execute.
  • Entry-level roles: emphasize hard skills you learned in school or projects. Soft skills matter but are harder to prove without work experience.

When in doubt, read the job description. Count the hard skills vs soft skills it mentions, that's your ratio.

Frequently asked questions

What are hard skills?

Hard skills are teachable, measurable abilities acquired through education, training, or practice. Examples include programming languages, data analysis, financial modeling, graphic design, and foreign languages. They can be tested or certified and are specific to a job or industry.

What are soft skills?

Soft skills are interpersonal and behavioral traits that affect how you work with others and approach tasks. Examples include communication, leadership, problem-solving, time management, and adaptability. They are harder to measure but apply across every role and industry.

Which matters more on a resume, hard or soft skills?

Both matter, but hard skills get you past the ATS and soft skills win the interview. Most job descriptions list required hard skills as keywords the ATS scans for. Soft skills differentiate you once a human reads your resume. The ideal resume demonstrates both.

How many skills should I put on a resume?

List 8-12 skills that are relevant to the target job. Prioritize hard skills the job description mentions, and include 2-3 soft skills you can back up with examples in your experience section.

Can soft skills be learned?

Yes. While some people have natural inclinations toward certain soft skills, all of them can be developed through practice, feedback, coaching, and intentional effort.

Should I list soft skills in a separate section?

Generally no. Listing "team player" or "good communicator" without evidence looks generic. Instead, demonstrate soft skills through your bullet points with quantified achievements.
