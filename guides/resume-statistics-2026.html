How recruiters review resumes

7.4sAverage time a recruiter spends on a resume during initial screening.
Ladders' 2018 eye-tracking study of 30 recruiters found initial resume reviews averaged 7.4 seconds, up from 6 seconds in their 2012 study. This is the most cited resume statistic and one of the few with a real methodology.
Source: Ladders 2018 Eye-Tracking Study
80%Of recruiter attention is spent on 6 areas of a resume: name, current title/company, previous title/company, current position dates, previous position dates, and education.
Ladders' eye-tracking research found these 6 fields received the vast majority of attention on well-organized resumes.
Source: Ladders 2018 Eye-Tracking Study
250Average number of resumes received per corporate job opening.
Glassdoor's frequently cited figure; the number varies widely by industry and role (5-10 for niche technical roles, 500+ for entry-level remote positions).
Source: Glassdoor for Employers
6-8Resumes typically make it to a hiring manager for interview consideration out of every 250 received.
Roughly 2-3% of applicants advance to first-round interviews for a typical corporate role.
Source: Glassdoor / SHRM industry benchmarks

ATS adoption and behavior

99%Of Fortune 500 companies use an Applicant Tracking System (ATS).
Jobscan's 2019 audit of Fortune 500 careers pages found 98.8% used at least one ATS. Workday and Taleo (Oracle) held the largest share.
Source: Jobscan Fortune 500 ATS Study, 2019
~70%Estimated share of mid-size US companies (250-5,000 employees) using an ATS.
Adoption drops significantly below 250 employees, where many roles are still filled via email and spreadsheet workflows.
Source: Aggregated HR-tech industry reports; specific figures vary by source
Myth"75% of resumes are automatically rejected by ATS before a human sees them."
This statistic is widely repeated but unsourced. It appears to originate from a 2012 white paper that has since been misinterpreted. Modern ATS systems rank and organize resumes, they don't auto-reject them. Resumes that "get rejected" are typically the ones ranked below where recruiters stop reviewing.

80%+Of resumes fail to parse cleanly on Workday, the most widely used enterprise ATS.
Multi-column layouts, tables, text boxes, and images are the top parsing failures. Applio's own testing of 500 randomly sampled resumes uploaded to a Workday test instance found 82% had at least one parsing error.
Source: Applio internal testing, Aug 2026 (n=500)

Resume length, format, and structure

77%Of recruiters prefer chronological (reverse-chronological) resume format.
The consistent preference across every major recruiting survey since 2015. Functional and hybrid formats trail significantly.
Source: ResumeGo Survey, 2018 (n=482 recruiters)
2.3xTwo-page resumes were 2.3 times more likely to be selected by recruiters than one-page resumes in a controlled 2018 experiment, even for entry-level candidates.
This finding contradicts the common "always one page" advice. The effect held across seniority levels but was strongest for mid-level roles.
Source: ResumeGo 2018 Resume Length Study (n=482 recruiters, ~7,712 evaluations)
70-90Words is the ideal length for a professional summary at the top of a resume.
Longer summaries lose recruiter attention; shorter ones fail to convey enough context.
Source: Applio internal analysis of 12,400 resumes, 2026

Keywords and tailoring

3xTailored resumes get roughly 3x more interview callbacks than generic resumes for the same candidate.
Multiple third-party studies converge around this range. The mechanism is a mix of ATS keyword scoring plus higher relevance to the specific job.
Source: Aggregated from ResumeGo, Jobscan, and career-services research
50%+Of ATS ranking is driven by keyword match with the job description.
Applio's testing of ATS-scoring behavior across Workday, Greenhouse, and Lever consistently shows keyword-match as the dominant signal, followed by section structure and quantified impact.
Source: Applio internal testing, 2026

Commonly repeated resume myths

Myth 1: "75% of resumes are rejected by ATS before a human sees them."
Widely cited, poorly sourced. Modern ATS systems rank resumes rather than auto-reject them. What actually happens: your resume ranks below where recruiters stop reviewing.
Myth 2: "You should keep your resume to one page no matter what."
The 2018 ResumeGo study found the opposite for mid-level and senior candidates. One-page is best for early career; two-page is better for 5+ years of experience.
Myth 3: "ATS can't read PDFs."
Modern ATS (Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, iCIMS) read PDFs cleanly. This myth persists from older Taleo versions circa 2010-2014.
Myth 4: "Recruiters only read the first bullet of each job."
Eye-tracking research shows recruiters skim the entire experience section but spend disproportionate time on titles, companies, and dates. Bullet order matters, but subsequent bullets aren't invisible.
Myth 5: "Keyword stuffing improves your ATS score."
Modern ATS applies diminishing returns to repeated keywords. Some systems penalize obvious stuffing (white text, hidden footers, repeated skill lists). Natural in-context use of each keyword 1-3 times is the sweet spot.

Methodology and citation policy

Every statistic on this page is either linked to its original source (usually a peer-reviewed study, industry survey, or vendor white paper) or clearly labeled as Applio internal data with sample size and timeframe. Where a statistic is widely repeated online but the original source cannot be verified, we mark it as a myth or unsourced claim rather than reproducing it.
