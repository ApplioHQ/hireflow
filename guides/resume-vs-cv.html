Resume vs CV: The Difference (US, UK, Europe, Academic)

Updated 2026-09-05 · 7 min read · Applio
In the US and Canada, a resume is a 1-2 page targeted marketing document for a specific job, while a CV (curriculum vitae) is a longer comprehensive academic record used for research and university roles. In the UK, Ireland, most of Europe, Australia, New Zealand, and India, "CV" is the everyday word for what Americans call a resume, and it's expected to be short (1-2 pages) and tailored. So: same word, different meaning by country.

The US definition: resume and CV are different documents

In the American usage, a resume and a CV are two distinct documents used in different contexts.

Resume (US)

  • Length: 1 page (early career) or 2 pages (10+ years experience)
  • Purpose: Marketing document tailored to a specific job
  • Content: Highlights of relevant experience, skills, and quantified achievements
  • Tone: Concise, active, results-focused
  • Used for: Nearly all industry, corporate, tech, finance, healthcare, and non-academic roles
  • Frequency of updates: Tailored for each application

CV (US)

  • Length: No upper limit; typically 3-5 pages early-career, 10+ pages senior faculty
  • Purpose: Comprehensive record of academic and professional history
  • Content: Publications, presentations, grants, teaching, research, honors, memberships
  • Tone: Formal, comprehensive, largely narrative-free
  • Used for: Academic positions, research fellowships, medical residencies, grant applications
  • Frequency of updates: Cumulative; grows over time rather than being tailored

The UK / European definition: CV = resume

Outside the US, "CV" is the standard word for what Americans call a resume. A UK CV, a German CV (Lebenslauf), and a French CV (curriculum vitae) all refer to the same 1-2 page tailored document.

If you're applying to a UK job and the listing asks for a CV, they mean a 1-2 page tailored document, not a 10-page academic record. Sending a US-style academic CV to a UK marketing role would look strange and unprofessional.

The one exception: UK, European, and Australian universities do use longer CVs for academic positions, similar to the US convention. But in industry, "CV" universally means the short document.

Country-by-country reference

United States and Canada

Resume for nearly all jobs. CV for academic, research, medical, and grant applications. The two words are not interchangeable in the US.

United Kingdom and Ireland

"CV" is the word for what Americans call a resume. Length: 1-2 pages. No photo. Include a personal statement (similar to a US professional summary) at the top.

Germany, Austria, Switzerland (German-speaking Europe)

The document is called a Lebenslauf. Tabular format with dates on the left and roles/education on the right. Photo expected. Personal details (date of birth, nationality) often included, though this is being phased out for anti-discrimination reasons.

France

Called a "CV" (pronounced "say-vay"). Photo expected. 1-2 pages. French employers often expect a formal cover letter (lettre de motivation) alongside.

Spain, Italy, Portugal

"CV" or "curriculum" is standard. Photo common. The Europass CV format is widely accepted and often preferred for public-sector and cross-border applications.

Netherlands, Belgium, Scandinavia

"CV" is standard. 1-2 pages. Photo optional and increasingly uncommon. Content similar to a US resume.

Australia and New Zealand

"CV" and "resume" are used interchangeably. 1-2 pages. Similar structure to US resume. No photo.

India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka

"Resume," "CV," and sometimes "biodata" are all used. Corporate roles expect a modern 1-2 page resume/CV. Biodata (an older format with personal details, photos, and family information) is still used in some traditional contexts and for marriage introductions.

UAE, Saudi Arabia, Middle East

"CV" is standard. Photo expected. 1-2 pages. Personal details (nationality, marital status) often included, though this varies by employer.

Japan and South Korea

Japan uses a standardized handwritten format called rirekisho for traditional roles, and a modern shokumu keirekisho for career-focused applications. South Korea also has standardized formats. Both differ significantly from Western CVs.

The academic CV specifically

An academic CV is fundamentally different from any resume format. It's structured around evidence of scholarly contribution rather than achievements from prior jobs. Standard sections, roughly in order:

  1. Contact information
  2. Education (with dissertation title, advisor, committee)
  3. Academic appointments (postdocs, faculty positions)
  4. Publications (peer-reviewed journal articles, book chapters, books, conference proceedings) — often broken into subsections
  5. Grants and funding (with dollar amounts and role: PI, Co-PI, contributor)
  6. Awards and honors
  7. Invited talks and presentations
  8. Teaching experience (courses taught, curriculum development)
  9. Advising / mentoring (PhD students, postdocs, undergrads)
  10. Service (peer review, editorial boards, committees)
  11. Professional memberships
  12. References (3-5 with full contact info)

Academic CVs grow throughout a career. A postdoc's CV is usually 3-5 pages; a senior tenured faculty CV can exceed 30 pages. The document is comprehensive rather than tailored.

Which one should you send?

Ask yourself two questions:

  1. What country are you applying in? If US or Canada, jump to question 2. If anywhere else, send a 1-2 page CV.
  2. Is this an academic, research, medical, or fellowship application? If yes, send a full academic CV. If no (industry, corporate, tech, startup, non-profit, government), send a resume.

When in doubt, follow the exact terminology the job posting uses. If the listing says "please submit your CV," send a CV formatted to their expectations. If it says "resume," send a resume.

For most people reading this guide, the answer is: send a resume. It's the right document for the vast majority of jobs anyone applies to, regardless of country (since "CV" outside the US just means resume).

Do ATS systems treat resumes and CVs differently?

No. Applicant Tracking Systems parse both the same way: they extract text, look for standard section headings (Experience, Education, Skills), and match your content against the job description's keywords. Whichever document you submit, the same rules apply:

  • Use standard section headings
  • Single-column layout
  • Common fonts (Arial, Calibri, Georgia)
  • No tables, text boxes, or images in the body
  • Match keywords from the job description
  • Save as PDF or DOCX

For academic CVs submitted through ATS-driven university hiring portals, the same rules apply, though academic hiring typically involves more human review and less automated filtering than corporate roles.

Frequently asked questions

Are a resume and a CV the same thing?

It depends on the country. In the US and Canada, they are different documents. In the UK, Europe, Australia, and India, "CV" is the everyday term for what Americans call a resume.

How long is a CV vs a resume?

US resume: 1-2 pages. US academic CV: 3-15+ pages. UK/European CV: same as a US resume, 1-2 pages.

Which should I send to a US employer?

For nearly all non-academic US jobs, send a resume. Reserve a full CV for academic, research, medical, or fellowship applications.

Which should I send to a UK employer?

Send a CV, but treat it like a US resume: 1-2 pages, tailored. UK employers do not expect an American-style academic CV for corporate roles.

What does CV stand for?

CV stands for curriculum vitae, Latin for "course of life."

Do you use both a resume and a CV?

Rarely. Academic job seekers sometimes maintain both. Most professionals only need one.

Does the ATS treat them differently?

No. Both are parsed the same way. Standard headings, clean formatting, and keyword matching apply to both.

Is a resume the same as a biodata?

No. Biodata is a longer document common in South Asia that includes personal details like date of birth and marital status. For modern corporate roles in India, use a resume or CV.

Do you include a photo on a CV?

Not in the US, UK, Ireland, Canada, or Australia. In much of continental Europe and the Middle East, a professional headshot is expected.

Is a CV more formal than a resume?

In the US, yes: an academic CV is more comprehensive and formal. Outside the US, CV and resume are the same document.

What is a Europass CV?

Europass is a standardized CV template developed by the EU. Widely used in continental Europe and often preferred for cross-border and public-sector applications.

Should I list references on a resume or CV?

On a resume: no. On an academic CV: yes, 3-5 references with contact info at the end.
