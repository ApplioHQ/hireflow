College Student Resume Examples & Template

Updated 2026-09-04 · Applio
A strong college student resume leads with your education (degree, major, GPA if 3.5+), then highlights coursework, projects, internships, and campus activities that show real skills. Every bullet should show what you actually did and any measurable result, even from group projects, volunteer work, or side projects. Keep it to one page.

What to include on a college student resume

  1. Contact info: Name, email, phone, LinkedIn, GitHub or portfolio if you have one
  2. Education: University, degree, expected graduation date, GPA (if 3.5+), relevant coursework
  3. Experience: Internships, part-time jobs, research, tutoring, campus roles
  4. Projects: Class projects, hackathons, personal builds, group work
  5. Skills: Technical tools, languages, software, certifications
  6. Activities and awards: Clubs, leadership roles, scholarships, competitions

College student resume bullet point examples

Internship / job experience:

  • Built and shipped a customer feedback dashboard used by 15+ team members, reducing weekly reporting time from 3 hours to 25 minutes.
  • Supported a team of 4 marketing associates by drafting 12 blog posts that generated 8,000+ organic visits in three months.
  • Analyzed 6 months of customer support data in Python and pandas, identifying the 3 issue categories responsible for 60% of tickets.

Projects and coursework:

  • Developed a full-stack event-planning web app (React, Node.js, PostgreSQL) as a semester project, used by 40+ students to coordinate campus events.
  • Led a 4-person team through a database systems capstone, delivering a working prototype and earning the highest project grade in the class of 60.
  • Placed 3rd of 45 teams at [University] Hackathon 2025 for a real-time transit tracking app built in 36 hours.

Campus leadership and activities:

  • Elected president of the Computer Science Club, growing active membership from 32 to 88 through weekly workshops and industry speaker events.
  • Tutored 15+ underclassmen in calculus and physics, contributing to an average grade improvement of a full letter.

Every bullet includes a specific action and a measurable outcome. Do the same with your own experiences.

Common college student resume mistakes

  • Listing coursework generically: "Data Structures, Algorithms, Databases" adds nothing. Only list courses if they directly match the job.
  • Describing group projects as duties: Say what you specifically owned and what the outcome was.
  • Including high school: Once you're in college, remove high school unless you're a freshman with a notable award.
  • Padding with soft skills: "Team player," "hard worker," and "detail-oriented" are meaningless. Show these through your bullets.
  • Not tailoring for the internship type: A finance internship and a software internship deserve two different resumes emphasizing different projects.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a resume as a college student?

Yes. You need one for internships, campus jobs, part-time roles, research assistantships, scholarships, and study-abroad applications. Build it early so you're never scrambling.

What if I don't have any work experience?

You have more than you think. Coursework projects, group assignments, campus leadership, volunteer work, tutoring, research, and side projects all count. Frame them the same way you'd frame a job.

Should I include my GPA?

Include your GPA if it's 3.5 or higher. Below that, drop it and lead with coursework, projects, and activities instead. GPA matters less once you have real work experience.

How long should a college student resume be?

One page. Recruiters expect a single page for students and early-career candidates. Cut anything not directly relevant.
