High School Student Resume Examples & Template

Updated 2026-09-16 · Applio
A good high school student resume leads with your education (school name, expected graduation, GPA if 3.0+), then highlights extracurriculars, volunteer work, part-time jobs, and any skills you've picked up. Every bullet should describe what you actually did and what happened as a result. Keep it to one page.

What to include on a high school student resume

  1. Contact info: Name, email, phone number, city and state
  2. Education: School name, expected graduation date, GPA (if 3.0+), honors or AP classes
  3. Experience: Part-time jobs, summer jobs, babysitting, tutoring, lawn care, freelance work
  4. Extracurriculars: Sports, clubs, student government, band, theater, debate
  5. Volunteer work: Community service, church groups, food banks, fundraisers
  6. Skills: Software, languages, certifications (CPR, food handler's card, etc.)

High school student resume bullet point examples

Part-time jobs:

  • Served 40+ customers per shift at a fast-paced restaurant, consistently receiving positive feedback from managers for speed and accuracy.
  • Operated a cash register and processed $1,500+ in daily transactions with zero shortages over a 6-month period.
  • Trained 3 new team members on store procedures and point-of-sale system within their first week.

Extracurriculars and leadership:

  • Elected captain of the varsity soccer team (22 players), organizing weekly practices and leading the team to a regional semifinal finish.
  • Served as treasurer of the National Honor Society chapter, managing a $2,400 annual budget for community service events.
  • Founded a coding club with 18 members, teaching introductory Python and HTML workshops every Friday.

Volunteer work:

  • Logged 200+ hours at the local food bank, sorting and distributing donations to 150+ families weekly.
  • Organized a school supply drive that collected 500+ items for an underfunded elementary school.

Every bullet includes a specific action and a measurable result. Do the same with your own experiences.

Common high school student resume mistakes

  • Writing "References available upon request": This wastes space. Employers know they can ask for references.
  • Listing every class you've taken: Only include classes that are relevant to the job or show advanced work (AP, Honors).
  • Using an unprofessional email: Create a simple email with your name. Avoid nicknames or numbers that look informal.
  • Vague descriptions: "Helped at the store" tells nothing. Say what you specifically did and what the result was.
  • Going over one page: One page is the standard. If you're filling space, tighten your descriptions rather than adding filler.

Frequently asked questions

Do high school students need a resume?

Yes. You need one for part-time jobs, summer jobs, volunteer positions, internships, and college applications. Having a resume ready means you can apply the moment an opportunity appears.

What do I put on a resume with no work experience?

School activities, volunteer work, clubs, sports, babysitting, tutoring, church or community roles, and personal projects all count. Focus on what you did and any result you can measure.

Should I include my GPA on a high school resume?

Include it if it's 3.0 or above. If your GPA is lower, lead with honors classes, awards, or extracurriculars instead.

How long should a high school student resume be?

One page, always. Employers and admissions officers expect a single page from high school students.
