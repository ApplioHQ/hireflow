How AI writes a better resume than most people write themselves

The problem with writing your own resume isn't grammar or vocabulary, it's perspective. Most people describe their own work in the language they used at the job, not the language a hiring manager scans for. AI is good at translating "I made the checkout page faster" into "Reduced checkout latency 62% by rebuilding the payment flow with server-side React, contributing to $180K in recovered annual revenue."

Where AI helps most

  • Strengthening weak bullets: Turning "responsible for managing X" into a strong action-verb bullet with a measurable outcome
  • Writing your summary: Compressing your background into a 2-3 sentence pitch that reads as a match for the specific role
  • Suggesting missing keywords: Identifying terms from the job description that should appear on your resume
  • Rewriting for a specific job: Adjusting emphasis, ordering, and language to match a specific posting

Where AI shouldn't take over

  • Deciding what to include or omit — that's your judgment about what matters for this role
  • Inventing accomplishments or numbers — those must come from you
  • Personality and voice for creative or executive roles — AI helps, but you have final say

The right way to use AI is as a fast, direct writing partner that gets you to a strong draft, then you review, edit, and add the nuances only you can add.
