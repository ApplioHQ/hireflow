What makes a resume bullet point strong?

Most resume bullets fail in the same way: they describe a duty, not an accomplishment. "Responsible for managing X" tells a recruiter what your job description said, not what you actually did. Strong bullets answer the unspoken question every recruiter has: so what?

The anatomy of a strong bullet point

Every high-quality resume bullet point has three parts:

  • Action verb (past tense): Built, Led, Grew, Reduced, Launched, Shipped, Secured, Designed
  • What you did: Specific task, project, or initiative, not a generic duty
  • Measurable outcome: A number, percentage, dollar amount, time saved, or scale that proves impact

The formula: [Action verb] + [specific work] + [by X% / $X / X faster / from A to B]

Why quantified bullets matter for ATS

Applicant Tracking Systems score resumes partly on the presence of numbers and specific keywords. A bullet with "reduced customer churn by 18%" scores higher than "improved customer retention" because it signals concrete, measurable work. Our ATS checker scores your resume's quantification rate alongside keyword matching.

Action verbs that perform best by category

  • Leadership: Led, Managed, Directed, Oversaw, Mentored, Coached, Spearheaded
  • Building / creating: Built, Designed, Developed, Launched, Architected, Shipped, Created
  • Improving / optimizing: Reduced, Increased, Improved, Streamlined, Accelerated, Cut, Drove
  • Collaboration: Partnered, Collaborated, Coordinated, Facilitated, Aligned, Unified
  • Analysis: Analyzed, Identified, Evaluated, Modeled, Forecasted, Researched
  • Revenue / growth: Grew, Generated, Secured, Closed, Expanded, Unlocked

What if I don't have numbers?

Most people underestimate what counts as a measurable result. Ask yourself: how many people did this affect? How long did it take before vs. after? How much did it cost the company? How many times per week/month did this happen? Even rough estimates ("~30 clients," "team of 6") are better than nothing. If there's genuinely no number, add scope or specificity instead: "Led redesign of the 7-step onboarding flow used by all new enterprise accounts."

Frequently asked questions

How many bullet points should each job on my resume have?

Most roles work best with 3-5 bullet points. Your most recent and relevant job can go up to 6. Older roles, especially those 10+ years ago, should have 2-3 at most. Every bullet should describe a specific accomplishment, not a generic duty.

Should I use first person on resume bullet points?

No. Omit "I." Start directly with the action verb. "Led a team of 5 engineers..." not "I led a team of 5 engineers..." This is a standard resume convention across every industry.

What tense should I use for resume bullet points?

Use past tense for previous roles. Use present tense for your current role. Mix creates confusion, so be consistent within each job.

Does Applio's AI invent results I didn't achieve?

No. The AI rewrites and strengthens the language in what you provide, it doesn't add numbers or accomplishments you didn't mention. It may prompt you to add a number if you didn't include one, but it won't fabricate results.
