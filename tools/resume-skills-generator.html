How to choose skills for your resume

The skills section is one of the most ATS-indexed parts of your resume and one of the most frequently done wrong. Most people either list too many generic skills or copy skills from a template that don't match the role they're applying for.

Hard skills vs. soft skills

Hard skills are specific, teachable, and measurable: Python, DCF modeling, Google Analytics, Figma. Soft skills like "communication" or "team player" are nearly useless in a skills list because every candidate claims them and ATS largely ignores them. Demonstrate soft skills through your bullet points instead of listing them.

How to find the right skills for a specific job

  • Read the job description carefully and note every specific tool, technology, or methodology mentioned
  • Check the "required" vs. "preferred" sections, required skills are the minimum bar, preferred ones are where you differentiate
  • Look at similar job postings from other companies to identify the consistent skills the role demands
  • Check LinkedIn profiles of people already in that role to see what they list

How to organize your skills section

Group skills into categories if you have many. For example, a software engineer might organize under: Languages (Python, Go, JavaScript), Frameworks (React, FastAPI), Infrastructure (AWS, Docker, Kubernetes), Tools (Git, Jira, Datadog). This is more readable than an undifferentiated list and helps ATS parse each group correctly.
