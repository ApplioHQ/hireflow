How to write a resume professional summary

A professional summary sits at the top of your resume and gives a recruiter the compressed version of why they should keep reading. It's two to four sentences that answer: who are you, what's your relevant experience, and what do you bring to this role?

What to include in a strong summary

  • Your title and years of experience: "Senior data analyst with 6 years..."
  • Your primary domain or industry: "...in financial services and healthcare..."
  • One or two specific accomplishments: "...built dashboards that reduced reporting time from 3 days to 4 hours..."
  • What you're looking for (optional): "...seeking a senior analytics role where I can own end-to-end data strategy."

What to avoid

  • Generic filler: "results-driven professional with strong communication skills"
  • Obvious statements: "seeking to use my skills in a challenging environment"
  • Long paragraphs: recruiters skim, keep it under 80 words
  • Personal pronouns: don't start with "I"

Tailor it for every application

A summary that matches the job description's language performs significantly better in ATS scoring. If the job says "data-driven decision making" and "cross-functional collaboration," those phrases belong in your summary if they genuinely apply to you.
