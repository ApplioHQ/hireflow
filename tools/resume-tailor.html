Why tailoring is the highest-ROI resume action you can take

Most job seekers think their resume isn't good enough. Usually, the resume is fine, it just isn't targeted. The same experience that impresses a hiring manager at one company looks irrelevant at another because it doesn't match the role's language or priorities.

How ATS keyword matching works

When you submit a resume through an ATS portal, the software parses your text and scores it against the job description's keywords. A resume that uses "cross-functional team leadership" scores higher for a role that mentions it than a resume that says "worked with different departments," even if the underlying work is identical. Our ATS checker shows you your exact score and the keywords you're missing.

What to change when tailoring

  • Professional summary: Rewrite the first two sentences to reflect the role's primary priorities
  • Bullet point emphasis: Lead with the accomplishments most relevant to this specific role
  • Keywords: Add missing keywords that genuinely apply to your experience, using the job's exact language
  • Skills section: Reorder to put the skills this role emphasizes at the top

What not to change

Don't add experience you don't have, inflate numbers you didn't achieve, or claim skills you lack. Tailoring is about framing and language, not fabrication. Beyond being dishonest, fabricated experience typically comes out in the interview.
